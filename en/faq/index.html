Frequently asked questions.

Everything you need to know before a property inspection or valuation.

Property inspections

Is a property inspection report mandatory?

Yes. For every residential lease in Belgium, whether in Brussels, Wallonia or Flanders, a detailed move-in inspection report (état des lieux / plaatsbeschrijving) is mandatory. It is drawn up with both parties present, while the property is empty or during the first month of occupancy, and attached to the lease.

Who pays for the inspection?

When the landlord and tenant jointly appoint the same expert, the cost is split equally. That is why our prices are shown per party. If one party hires its own expert, it pays that expert alone.

What happens if there is no move-in inspection?

The tenant is then presumed to have received the property in the condition in which they return it. In practice, the landlord can hardly claim for any damage at move-out. The inspection report protects both parties.

Do we have to use an expert?

No, the landlord and tenant may draw it up themselves. An independent expert, however, provides a neutral, complete record with photos, which carries weight if there is a dispute over the rental deposit.

Do I need to attend the inspection?

Yes, the landlord and tenant must attend or be represented, for example by the letting agency or by someone holding a power of attorney.

How long does an inspection take?

Allow about 1 hour for an apartment and 1.5 to 2 hours for a house.

What should I prepare for the inspection?

Keys to all doors, access to the meters (water, gas, electricity) to record the readings, and the electricity switched on so that lighting and appliances can be tested.

What is the move-out inspection for?

The property is compared with the move-in report to identify any tenant damage beyond normal wear and tear. It is the basis for releasing the rental deposit.

Does the inspection report have to be registered?

Yes, the landlord registers the move-in report together with the lease, free of charge, with the FPS Finance (MyRent).

Property valuation

How much does a valuation cost?

€300 including VAT for a house or apartment. For an investment property or a larger building, the price is on quotation.

When should I ask for a valuation?

For a sale, a purchase, an inheritance or gift, a separation or division of assets, or a mortgage application.
